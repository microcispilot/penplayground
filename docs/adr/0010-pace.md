# ADR-0010: Pace — a teacher's rhythm at 1×, one host-set number that moves voice, beats and board together

Status: accepted · the 1× speed was lowered to 0.85 by ADR-0047 (2026-09-24) · 2026-09-17

## Context
The owner's words: "the tutor is moving in a huge pace." Sentence-level TTS
butted every sentence against the next, the board wrote at 11 chars/s, and
Fish's speed 1.0 reads like a newsreader. Learners also differ: a revision
session wants 1.3×, a first contact with a hard topic wants 0.75× — and
guests in a room must hear exactly what the host hears. Replay needs the same
control, like the speed menu on a video, without the voice going up in pitch.
ADR-0002 fixes the constraint: the audio clock is the master clock; board and
captions follow it, never the reverse.

## Decision
1. **One number, one place.** `packages/contracts/src/pace.ts` holds every
   constant the pace touches, with the reason for each value:
   - Fish `prosody.speed` at 1× = **0.95** (the point where a listener stops
     hearing the voice "keeping up"), scaled `0.95 × pace`, clamped to Fish's
     0.5–2.
   - Beat after a sentence = **400 ms**; after a check-in question or the
     sentence that carries a board title = **700 ms**. Divided by the pace.
   - Handwriting = **10 chars/s** (was 11); typewriter 40 chars/s. Multiplied
     by the pace. `TIMING.handwritingCps` now reads from `PACE`.
   - Presets **0.75 / 0.9 / 1 / 1.15 / 1.3**; the wire accepts 0.5–2
     (`Pace` Zod schema); the server clamps, never rejects, a preset.
2. **The beat is audio.** The `SayPipeline` appends the paced silence to each
   sentence as ordinary 120 ms PCM frames on the same clock, so the pause is
   in the ledger, in the replay and in the export by construction, and the
   audio clock stays master. The kind of beat (sentence / check / title) is
   decided once the speech has streamed; because a `check` or `title` cue is
   emitted *after* the sentence it belongs to, the decision waits — bounded by
   300 ms — for the next cue. The speech is already on its way; only the tail
   waits, so the wait is never audible. The last 3 ms of speech ramp to zero
   before the beat so the seam cannot click.
   The check-in card, and the room's `checking` mode, belong to the end of the
   *words*, not of the beat: the conductor reveals the check at
   `duration − checkGap` (falling back to the say's end), so a learner who
   answers during the beat is graded rather than taken as asking a question.
3. **Pace is room state.** `RoomState.pace`; client message `set_pace` (host
   only — guests get `NOT_HOST`); every participant receives it in `state`;
   the ledger records `{ kind: 'pace', t, pace, participantId }`. A spoken
   "slow down" from the host steps one preset down.
4. **Takes effect from the next sentence.** The pipeline reads the pace when a
   sentence starts synthesis. The sentence in flight finishes at its own
   speed, and so do up to `lookahead` (3) sentences already synthesised and
   banked on the clients — the change is heard at most two sentences after the
   current one. Re-taking banked sentences is a follow-up (tasks/todo.md).
5. **The client follows.** The conductor reads `state.pace`, captures it when a
   sentence starts (`sayPace`) and passes `rate = pace × playbackRate` to
   `BoardPort.execute`; the board's natural writing time is divided by the
   rate, the floor and stretch cap apply to the scaled time. `with:` ops are
   still paced to the audio duration.
6. **Replay: a playback rate on top, pitch preserved.** `ReplaySession` plays
   through `MediaSayPlayer` (`packages/voice/src/client/media-player.ts`):
   each say's PCM becomes one WAV blob in an `HTMLMediaElement` with
   `playbackRate` and `preservesPitch = true` — the browser's time-stretcher,
   the same one a video player's speed menu uses. Web Audio's
   `AudioBufferSourceNode.playbackRate` resamples (a higher voice), so the live
   `PcmPlayer` stays live-only. The player keeps the conductor's clock
   contract: `onSayEnd(sayId, recordedMs)`, `clock.offsetMs` on the say's own
   timeline; the conductor divides by the playback rate for wall time. The
   recorded pace is replayed too: ledger `pace` entries become `state` updates
   at the sentence they applied to. The export renderer is untouched
   (`ExportClock`, ledger durations, 1×).
7. **UI.** A "1×" pill in the bottom bar (`PaceMenu`) opens a menu of presets
   (`aria-pressed`, arrow keys, Escape returns focus); guests see it disabled
   with "Only the host sets the pace". The choice is remembered per device
   (`pen.pace`, platform storage) and sent as `set_pace` right after the host
   joins their next session. Replay has the same control labelled Speed
   (`pen.replay-rate`).

## Consequences
- 1× is slower than before by design: ~0.95 speed, +400 ms per sentence,
  −10 % board speed. Lessons planned for 14 min run ≈ 15.
- ~35 KB of silence per sentence in the ledger at 44.1 kHz (400 ms).
- Replay between says has the media element's start latency (tens of ms)
  instead of the sample-exact seam of the PcmPlayer; acceptable for a replay,
  not for a live room.
- Verified by ear: `pnpm --filter @pen/api listen:pace` writes
  `.pen-data/samples/pace-{0.75,1,1.3}.wav` and prints durations and median
  F0 (durations scale with the pace; the fundamental does not, because Fish
  synthesises each speed natively).
