# Glossary (domain model)

Use these words exactly, in code, docs and UI copy.

- **Topic** — what the learner asked to learn, as they said it ("I want to learn Swift fundamentals"). Normalised by the registry into a **canonical knowledge id**.
- **Knowledge Pack** — Onten's compiled, rights-cleared artifact for a topic scope. Layered: `shared_public_base` < `tenant_overlay` < `audience_overlay` < `user_overlay`.
- **AnswerContext** — what Onten hands the model for one question: status (`sufficient | partial | conflict | stale | missing`), one primary unit, 3–5 evidence spans, typed/derived facts, constraints, `modelContext`. Never an answer.
- **Selection band** — bounded, non-sensitive learner level (`beginner | intermediate | advanced`) that may change which units Onten selects while still sharing the memo. The only personalisation allowed in a shared key.
- **Expert** — an AI human persona from the catalog: name, role, biography, interaction style, portrait, `voice_id`, mandatory AI disclosure. Persona shapes manner, never capability.
- **Visitor** — a participant without an account (`anonymous`). Gets a taste and never a cost (ADR-0040): prepared lessons without limit, taught by one of the free plan's two experts, questions heard but not answered by the model, nothing to keep. Every feature rule may say what a visitor gets (`FeatureRule.anonymous`), and it wins for them over plan and platform.
- **Custom session** — a session on a topic nobody has prepared, prepared for this learner: the expensive path. One per free account over its life (`PEN_FREE_CUSTOM_SESSIONS`, counted in `participants.custom_sessions`), unlimited when paying, none for a visitor.
- **Default expert** — who sits in the search box before anything is typed. A paying account's own choice (`participants.default_expert_id`), else one the plan includes chosen at random once per visit; the free plan's two are Elena Ruiz and Soren Vale (`FREE_EXPERTS`).
- **Action** — one decision a visitor makes outside a live session — Start, a card, Upgrade, a plan, Sign in, like, save, share (`ActionName`, ADR-0038). A closed list of codes sent to PostHog with the screen; never a word the visitor typed. Distinct from an **interaction**, which is a session's own record and travels to its ledger.
- **Session** — one classroom instance: a topic, an expert, a host, 0–11 guests, a lesson, a timeline, a transcript, a recording.
- **Host** — the participant who started the session. Only the host can pause, resume, end, or invite.
- **Guest** — any other participant. Listens, watches, talks to the other people, and asks the expert by **raising a hand**; is heard by the expert only while holding the floor the expert gave them (ADR-0037).
- **Hand** — a guest's request for the floor, queued oldest first in `RoomState.hands`. The expert calls the first at the end of the sentence it is on (the **invitation**, on the `floor` thread), gives them the floor (`RoomState.invited`), and lets them go by name if they say nothing for `HAND_WAIT_MS` or lower it.
- **Discussion** — `LiveMode.discussing`: the host paused the class to talk among themselves. The lesson holds at the sentence, the expert waits and hears nobody, hands still queue. Host-only, both ways.
- **Lesson** — the ordered list of **segments** the expert intends to teach for this session. Produced by the planner from the pack (or reused via the memo).
- **Comment** — a paragraph under a saved session, the way YouTube has them (ADR-0044): everyone who can open the session reads the thread; an account writes one (`comments` flag, `anonymous: false`); its author and the session's host can delete it, softly (`deleted_at`). Plain text, up to 1,000 characters, ten a minute. Not a room's **chat**, which is between the people in a live room and never leaves it.
- **Watch page** — the saved session's page (`/sessions/:id`), in YouTube's shape (ADR-0044): board, title, the expert as a channel row with the actions beside it, a description box, comments, and *Up next* on the right. The board is the **player** (ADR-0045): pressed, it starts the lesson again as a fresh session in place (the live room, `SessionPlayer` at `layout="inline"`); a press on the board pauses and resumes; **Full view** is the player at the whole viewport. Visibility is a paid host's control (`session_visibility`); delete, the questions asked and the insights are an account host's.
- **Board surface** — the board a lesson is taught on (`BoardSurfaceId`: `auto`, whiteboard, blackboard, green board, Ivory, Smoked glass). `auto` is not a board: it is the whiteboard by day and the blackboard at night, and it is the only surface a free learner may select (ADR-0034). Each pinned surface names its own **colour**, the one ink it refuses (ADR-0041).
- **Writing tool** — chalk or marker (`BoardTool`), chosen on its own and usable on any surface; `auto` is the surface's natural tool (marker on a light board, chalk on a dark one). Stamped on `<html>` as `data-tool`.
- **Ink** — the colour the expert writes in (`InkId`: black, white, red, blue, green, yellow, pink), one palette for both tools and tuned per surface in `tokens.css`. The one rule: never the surface's own colour — disabled in Settings, never painted, and the stored choice is kept (ADR-0041).
- **Segment** — one teaching beat (≈ 30–120 s): a goal, speech, board work, optionally a check-in. Progress dots in the bottom bar are segments.
- **Plan opening** — the title, the promise and segment 1: the part of a lesson plan the model writes first, and everything segment 1's call needs. The room takes it while the rest of the outline is still being written and sends segment 1 against it, so the two calls overlap; nothing is spoken until the whole plan has landed (ADR-0019).
- **Cue** — the atomic unit of the live stream. Kinds: `say` (a sentence for the voice), `board` (one board op anchored to a `say`), `caption`, `check` (a question to the learner), `state` (room state change), `note` (pinned "You asked"). Cues are deterministic; every client renders the same cue the same way.
- **Anchor** — the relationship between a board op and speech: `with:<sayId>` (start when that sentence starts, finish by its end), `after:<sayId>` (start when it ends), `now`.
- **Conductor** — the client engine that plays cues in sync. Audio clock is the master clock. Owns pause/resume/barge-in locally.
- **Pace** — the one number that sets how fast the expert teaches (`RoomState.pace`; presets 0.75× / 0.9× / 1× / 1.15× / 1.3×, accepted 0.5–2). 1× is a patient teacher: Fish `prosody.speed` 0.85 (ADR-0047; the learner still sees 1×), a 400 ms beat after each sentence, 700 ms after a check-in question or a board title, handwriting at 10 chars/s. Pace scales the voice, the beats (÷ pace) and the board rate (× pace) together; the beats are audio, so the audio clock stays master. Host-set, broadcast in `state`, recorded in the ledger (`kind: 'pace'`); applies from the next sentence synthesised. Replay adds a **playback rate** on top (pitch-preserving, like a video's speed menu).
- **Delivery cue** — how a sentence is *said*, as distinct from what it says (ADR-0047). The sentence's `tone` becomes a Fish S2.1 bracket cue in front (`[warm]`, `[curious]`…); inline, the model may write a named few (`[emphasis]`, `[break]`, `[long-break]`, `[soft tone]`, `[whispering]`, `[chuckling]`, `[laughing]`, `[sighing]`). `splitDelivery` gives every say two texts: `text`, with every cue removed, which is all captions, recap, transcript and board ever see; and `spoken`, with the vetted cues kept, which the voice receives. Split in the room's `pushCue`, the one door every sentence goes through.
- **Setting** — a feature flag that carries a value instead of a bit (ADR-0048): `ChoiceRule` has a flag's axes plus **participants**, answers for named accounts, and resolves participant → visitor → cell → plan → platform → default. Stored in the same document as the flags, with the same revision, history and rollback; edited beside them in the console. The first is `voice_engine` (`cartesia` | `fish`, default Cartesia).
- **Voice binding** — what a session gets from `VoiceService.bind(who)` (ADR-0048): one engine's synthesizer behind its own store of taught lessons, and that engine's voice for every expert. Decided once when the session is created and never revisited, so a change reaches the next session and one session never mixes engines. A named engine the server has no key for falls to one it has, and `voice.engine_fallback` says so. The binding is recorded (`room.voice_engine`, the ledger's `voice_engine` entry, `session_stats.voice_engine`, PostHog `voice.engine`) so every report can group by it.
- **Absent room** — a live room with nobody seated (ADR-0049). The host's socket going pauses the lesson and throws away what was banked; `emptySince` starts, and the sweeper ends the room after `ABSENT_ROOM_MS` (three minutes) of absence, whatever its age. A reconnect inside the grace finds the lesson paused where it was.
- **Recognizer witness** — the rule that the browser's speech recognizer is believed only while the microphone confirms someone speaking, whenever a voice is coming out of the speakers (ADR-0046, `RecognizerGuard`). The recognizer has no echo cancellation and transcribes the expert as readily as the learner; the microphone is echo-cancelled and harmonic. A transcript with no witness is dropped before it can interrupt and counted as `echo_dropped`.
- **Turn** — one learner utterance handled by the brain: perceive (transcript + room state) → decide (answer / clarify / defer / check) → act (cues) → verify (did the learner continue?).
- **Interrupt (barge-in)** — the learner speaks while the expert is speaking. Confirmed after 240 ms of voiced speech; playback fades within 20 ms; the lesson is paused at the current cue.
- **Resume point** — the exact cue (and sentence offset) the lesson continues from after a turn or a host pause. Never a restart of the segment.
- **Check-in** — a question the expert asks the learner to verify understanding. Graded only against admitted evidence; provisional evidence can never grade (`mayAuthorizeConsequentialDecision=false`).
- **Preparation** — the topic-miss path: outline → sources → progressive compile. `interactive` resolves when the first useful, provisional context exists; `background` resolves when the qualified pack exists.
- **Recording ledger** — the append-only list of timestamped cues, audio chunks and participant events from which replay and export are reconstructed.
- **Replay** — starting a saved lesson again as a fresh live session of your own (ADR-0035): same topic, expert, band and language, reusing the lesson memo and the voice store, with your own questions and your own recording. What "Replay" on a card, a shelf row or a saved page does. Never a playback of somebody else's hour.
- **Room** — a session at least one guest took a seat in (`guests > 0`, counted from `session_visits`). Recorded whole for its host, who alone watches or exports it; everyone in it is told. Never replayed and never a catalogue card (ADR-0035).
- **Recording** — a session's ledger played back deterministically through the conductor, in the order it was heard (`recordingOrder`), with the host's own words captioned over the sentences that answered them. The host's alone: served to nobody else, ever. "Watch my recording" on the saved page.
- **Export** — an MP4 rendered from a recording (board frames + mixed audio) for download, by its host. Two **variants**: `full` (the session as lived, questions included) and `lesson` (every turn left out).
- **App shell** — the header plus the persistent left sidebar, around every screen except the live session and the replay (ADR-0015). **Rail** — its 72 px collapsed form; **drawer** — its slide-over form under 1024 px.
- **Shelf** — one of the learner's own lists: **Learn later** (saved), **Liked**, **History**. Keyed by participant id, so an anonymous participant has real shelves on that device; a Google sign-in adopts them onto the account exactly as it adopts sessions.
- **Save** — putting a session on the Learn later shelf (`session_saves`). **Like** — the public approval of a session (`session_likes`); exactly one per participant, counted on the card by the denormalised `sessions.likes`.
- **Visit** — a seat taken in a session (`session_visits`): who, which session, `host` or `guest`, first and last join. History is the participant's visits, most recent seat first.
- **Plan** — a billing tier: `free`, `plus`, `classroom`. **Entitlement** — a capability a plan grants (`rooms`, `export`, `no_ads`, `premium_voices`).
- **Key owner** — whose budget a provider call belongs to, and therefore which of the four OpenAI keys runs it: a `PlanCode` (the HOST'S plan, for everything a session does, background card and thumbnail included) or `platform` (`OPENAI_API_KEY_PLATFORM`, for work belonging to no learner — backfills, probes, prewarming). Keys never fall back to one another.
- **Session meta** — the card copy (description, keywords, category) for one session, produced by one background structured-output call after the plan exists (ADR-0013). Never on the critical path. Its **thumbnail** is a second, independent background call.
- **Thumbnail** — a session's picture: one `gpt-image-1` generation, 1536 × 1024, from the session title *and its subject*, stored as `source.png` with `thumb.webp` (640 × 360) and `og.jpg` (1200 × 630) derived from it (ADR-0021, ADR-0022). One generation per lesson, reused by every later session on the same topic. `thumbnail` on the record is null until it is ready; the calm placeholder shows until then. Sessions taught before ADR-0022 still carry the PNG pair, and before ADR-0021 a hand-drawn `thumb.svg`; both are still served and neither is written again.
- **Subject** (of a thumbnail) — the one physical thing a camera could be pointed at for a lesson, named in English by the same call that writes the card copy and dropped into the image prompt (ADR-0022). A camera cannot point at an abstraction: given one it photographs a diagram and letters it with invented words. Empty means the title-only prompt.
- **Video ad** — a skippable in-stream video (Google IMA, VAST tag from the server) shown over the board between segments on the free plan, or while a topic miss is prepared. Never spoken by the expert. **Ad slot** — `boundary` or `preparation`. **Ad tag** — the VAST/VMAP URL that names the demand source (`PEN_AD_TAG_URL`).
- **Stage sample** — one timed step of a session on the session clock (`intake`, `resolve`, `context`, `prepare`, `llm`, `intent`, `image`, `tts`, `stt`, `board`, `turn`, `ad`, `join`, `leave`): start, duration, ok, and content-free `meta` (ids, counts, first-token / first-chunk times, `reused`, `savedUsd`). A ledger entry (`metric`).
- **Cost line** — one priced unit of provider work (`llm` tokens in / cached / out, `intent` tokens in, `image` prompt tokens in / picture tokens out, `tts` bytes, `stt` seconds, `search` requests, `onten` requests, and the `ads` revenue credit) with its USD from the single price table. A ledger entry (`cost`).
- **Reaction** — one of eight fixed emoji a participant sends without taking the floor (ADR-0019). A broadcast and nothing else: it never touches the lesson, the expert, the board or the audio path, is rate-limited to one per participant per 600 ms, and is refused while an ad is on screen.
- **Session panel** — the room's right-hand column (ADR-0019): everyone on the call, the chat, and the box that writes into it. Folds away from a chevron on its own edge; below 1024 px it comes over the board as a drawer.
- **Chat** — what the **people** in the room say to each other (`ClientChat` / `ServerChat`). Modelled on the reaction: the room stamps it, rate-limits it to one line per participant per 400 ms, refuses it while an ad is on screen, broadcasts it to everyone including its sender — and nothing else in the session reads it. The expert never sees it, it takes no floor, reaches no model and costs nothing. Counted in the ledger as a length, never as words. Asking the expert something is speaking, not typing — and in a room, what was spoken to the expert appears in the chat under the speaker's name (`kind: 'question'`, marked "to Dario"), because the board never carries a learner's name or words (ADR-0035).
- **Interaction event** — what a participant did (spoke a question, interrupted, paused, skipped an ad, reacted, sent a chat line, toggled captions or the mic…) or was shown (screen, phase, note, check, ad, recap, first audio, answer start, board op done), reported by the client and stamped by the server. A ledger entry (`interaction`).
- **Telemetry port** — the small interface (`sample`, `cost`, `error`) instrumented modules write to; `SessionMetrics` implements it per session (ledger + PostHog), `NullMetrics` in tests.
- **Session telemetry** — the summary computed from a session's ledger: totals, latency percentiles (question → first audio, model first token, voice first chunk, hearing final, barge-in), cost by component, reuse, and the stages, interactions and errors themselves. Served host-only; rendered by the **Insights** tab.
- **Turn latency** — the learner's final words (final transcript or typed question) → the first audible chunk of the expert's reply (the acknowledgement), measured server-side.
- **Canonical id** — the Onten registry's `${lang}.${slug}` for a topic (`en.how-transformers-work-in-llms`); groups same-intent sessions for reuse statistics.
- **Reuse** — work a session served from earlier sessions instead of generating: a registry pack hit (no preparation), lesson memo segments (no model call), an Onten speculation hit, a cached intake translation. Each carries `savedUsd`; `freshEquivalentUsd` is what the session would have cost with zero reuse.
- **Lesson memo** — the persona's taught lesson for a topic scope, band and language (plan + cues per segment + what each cost), grown segment by segment as sessions get further; the next learner generates only what is missing. **Pen's own cache of Pen's own model output** — it lives in `packages/session-engine`, never in `packages/onten`, and it is not Onten's memo (below).
- **Canonical Question Memo** — Onten's memo (CTX-MEMO-01): the *selection* a question already made — which knowledge units answer it, and what each scored — remembered so a repeat skips retrieval and the selector entirely. It never caches an answer, it is keyed by topic and **selection band** (never by a learner), and a stored selection is re-admitted against the live packs on every hit. Lives in `packages/onten`; reported as `metrics.memoHit`.
- **Latency budget** — `ONTEN_LATENCY_BUDGET_MS = 20`: what one Onten `query` may take, end to end, from the host's call to the AnswerContext in its hand. Onten's own number. Measured on every call (`metrics.overBudget`, `runtime.latency()`) and gated at 20,000 units by `packages/onten/test/latency.test.ts`.

- **Runtime setting** — a choice about how the deployment runs (which model teaches, who classifies a turn, what a day may cost) that can be changed without a deploy (ADR-0025). Named by its environment variable, defined once by the environment schema, and resolved through three tiers: an **environment pin** (a variable the operator actually set on that box) beats the **stored document**, which beats the **compiled-in default**. Not a secret, not an address, not anything whose wrong value loses data — the ADR names every exclusion.
- **Runtime configuration document** — the JSON of stored overrides, one singleton row in `runtime_config_state`, with a **revision** that a save must carry or be refused. Held in memory by every API process and cached at `PEN_DATA_DIR/runtime-config.json`; a failed read keeps the **last known good** one rather than falling back to defaults, so an outage never changes how the product behaves. A revision is never rewritten: a **rollback** writes an old document forward as a new revision, recording which one it restored.
- **Setting scope** — when a change reaches the product, and therefore where the code reads it: `request` (the next request), `session` (read once as a room is built, so a lesson never changes half way through) or `restart` (built into a service at boot — the speech engine, the voice store, the model adapters).
- **Operations console** — `apps/admin` (ADR-0026): the settings screen and its revision history, on a hostname of its own, behind the Google accounts named in `PEN_ADMIN_EMAILS`. Statistics pages land beside it, and the Features screen (ADR-0036).
- **Feature flag** — a rule deciding whether one part of the product exists for a learner, by their **plan** and their **platform** (ADR-0036): a default, an answer per plan, an answer per platform (the two AND when both speak) and an answer per exact cell. Named in `FeatureName` (`prepare_new_topics`, `quick_start`, `rooms`, `recording_playback`, `session_download`, `ads`, `chat`, `reactions`, `captions`, `google_sign_in`, `email_sign_in`); the compiled-in rule is what the product does with nothing stored. A room is built with its flags and keeps them; the client is told its own cell by `GET /api/me/features` and decides nothing.
- **Platform** — where a learner is: `web`, `desktop-mac`, `desktop-windows`, `desktop-linux`, `ios`, `android`. Named by the client in the `x-pen-platform` header on every request; anything unparseable is `web`. The phones are listed before they exist so a rule can be set for them.
- **Preparation gate** — the refusal a topic miss meets when the host's `prepare_new_topics` is off: `402 PREPARATION_REQUIRED`, before a row is written or a room built, with the way to upgrade and the lessons that are ready (`ready`).
